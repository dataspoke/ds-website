Section 1

Where firms lose cases and hours

Most firms do not lose a prospective client on the law. They lose them in the gaps between tools. The first firm to respond clearly usually gets the call back, and the hours spent retyping and reconciling come straight out of billable time. These five gaps show up in almost every firm we talk to.

  • A lead sits in an inbox overnight. The web form emails a shared address, and nobody owns that address on a Friday evening.
  • Intake data is retyped into the case system. The same name, contact details and matter facts get keyed into intake, then practice management, then billing, with a fresh chance of a typo each time.
  • Nobody can say which ad source signs cases. Ad platforms report clicks, intake reports consultations, and nothing ties a signed engagement back to the campaign that produced it.
  • Partners argue over whose report is right. Each report pulls from a different system, so the Monday meeting starts with reconciling numbers instead of using them.
  • Decisions made on calls are lost. A client agrees to a strategy on a video call, and three weeks later nobody can find what was said or who said it.

Where the money leaks

A home service company can spend heavily on ads, lead vendors and canvassing and still not know what a paying customer costs. The money rarely leaks in one big place. It leaks in five ordinary ones.

  • A lead sits until Monday. It came from an ad, the web form or a lead vendor on Saturday, and nobody saw it until the office opened.
  • Office staff retype leads into the CRM. Vendor leads arrive by email, someone copies them in by hand, and some never make it at all, so nobody calls them and nobody counts them.
  • Ad reports count conversions sales can't find. The ad platform reports one lead count. The CRM shows fewer. Nobody can say which number is true.
  • Close rate by source and by rep is a guess. The lead source field is filled in by hand, everyone fills it in differently, and half the options mean the same thing.
  • Month-end means exporting four reports. Ad spend, payroll, sales and accounting live in separate systems, so someone spends a day lining them up and hoping they match.
